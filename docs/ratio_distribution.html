Why this matters for bootstrapping: each click of "Draw new sample" is a fresh Monte Carlo draw from the same population — exactly the resampling logic behind the bootstrap. Notice how much the histogram's shape and spread change from sample to sample, especially when μ is near 0. At μ = 0, X/Y follows a Cauchy distribution: it has no defined mean or variance, because Y keeps landing arbitrarily close to zero and blowing the ratio up. That's a case where the usual bootstrap standard-error intuition breaks down.
