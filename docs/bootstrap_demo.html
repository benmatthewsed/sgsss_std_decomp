A hands-on guide for applied researchers

What would your estimate look like, if you'd sampled again?

The nonparametric bootstrap answers that question without a second data collection wave. It treats your one sample as a stand-in for the population, draws new samples from it with replacement, and recomputes your statistic each time — building up a picture of how much that statistic would bounce around if the world handed you a different sample.

Why this is allowed to work

Classical formulas for a standard error (like s / √n for a mean) assume a particular sampling distribution. The bootstrap sidesteps that assumption: it treats the sample itself as the best available stand-in for the population, and lets resampling reveal the statistic's variability directly — useful for medians, trimmed means, ratios, or anything without a tidy textbook formula.

It is not magic. If your original sample is small, biased, or a poor representation of the population you care about, the bootstrap will faithfully reproduce those flaws — it cannot manufacture information that was never collected.
