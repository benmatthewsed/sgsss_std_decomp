Example by-hand - Crime Rates in Scotland vs Rest of UK

Question 1

Here is the data for two Local Authority Areas (LAA) in Scotland.
The crude birth rate is calculated as the number of births per 1000 people.

Calculate the birth rate for each LAA

LAA n_people n_women15to44 n_births birth_rate
Glasgow City 650300 161105 6150 ?
Stirling 94210 17844 659 ?

Solution 1.

LAA n_people n_women15to44 n_births birth_rate
Glasgow City 650300 161105 6150 9.46
Stirling 94210 17844 659 7.00

Question 2

We can decompose this into two components: the number of births per “woman of child bearing age”(Scotland uses ages 15-44)“, and the proportion of the population that are women aged 15-44.

Calculate these below, and then multiply them to demonstrate to yourselves that this returns the same rates as the previous question.

LAA n_people n_women15to44 n_births births / w15-44 w15-44 / people birth_rate
Glasgow City 650300 161105 6150 A1 B1 A1 * B1
Stirling 94210 17844 659 A2 B2 A2 * B2

Solution 2.

LAA n_people n_women15to44 n_births births / w15-44 * 1000 w15-44 / people birth_rate
Glasgow City 650300 161105 6150 38.174 0.248 9.47
Stirling 94210 17844 659 36.931 0.189 6.98

  • The proportion of the population comprised of women aged 15-44 was the same in both populations (i.e., at the average of the two)
  • The rates of births in women aged 15-44 were the same in both populations (i.e., at the average of the two)

A1 * ((B1+B2)/2)
A2 * ((B1+B2)/2)
and
B1 * ((A1+A2)/2)

Solution 6. 

p01 <- p01 |>
  mutate(
    factorA =  n_births/n_women15to44,
    factorB =  n_women15to44/n_people
  )

library(DasGuptR)
dres <- dgnpop(x = p01, 
       pop = "LAA",
       factors = c("factorA","factorB"))

Question 8

We can split the birth rate further, into the proportion of women:

\[ Rate = \frac{\text{women}}{\text{people}} \cdot \frac{\text{women 15-44}}{\text{women}} \cdot \frac{\text{births}}{\text{women 15-44}} \]

p01a <- data.frame(
  LAA = c("Glasgow City","Stirling"),
  n_people = c(650300,94210),
  n_women = c(332054, 48731),
  n_women15to44 = c(161105,17844),
  n_births = c(6150,659)
)

Solution 7. 

p01a <- p01a |> mutate(
  fa = n_births/n_women15to44,
  fb = n_women15to44/n_women,
  fc = n_women/n_people
)

dgnpop(p01a,
       pop="LAA",
       factors=c("fa","fb","fc")) |>
  dg_table()

The Gender Earnings-Gap

In 2025 in the UK, the total weekly earnings per working age man was £179. The total weekly earnings per working aged woman was £7.

This difference could be due to:

  • a greater number of men in the “labour force” (people who are employed or actively seeking employent. it excludes students etc).
  • more employment for men in the labour force compared to women in the labour force
  • men working more hours per work than women (i.e., fewer part time roles)
  • a higher average hourly wage for men compared to women (this is the “gender pay gap”, unadjusted for specific role)

You can find this data for a whole load of different countries at https://ilostat.ilo.org/data/?cat_mode=subject, if you would like to do this for somewhere else.
